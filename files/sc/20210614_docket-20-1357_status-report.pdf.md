## EPA'S STATUS REPORT

Pursuant to this Court's order of February 12, 2021, Respondents Administrator Michael Regan and the United States Environmental Protection Agency ('EPA') provide the following status report.  As explained below, EPA's review of the challenged rulemaking is ongoing.  Accordingly, EPA recommends that these consolidated cases continue to be held in abeyance pending completion of that review.

1. These consolidated cases involve challenges to a final EPA action entitled 'Oil and Natural Gas Sector: Emission Standards for New, Reconstructed, and Modified Sources Review,' 85 Fed. Reg. 57,018 (Sept. 14, 2020) ('2020 Rule' or 'the Rule').  The 2020 Rule sought to repeal aspects of EPA's 2012 and 2016

-- Page Break --

rulemakings concerning the regulation of pollutants, including methane, from the oil and gas industry.

2. While merits briefing of these challenges was ongoing, President Joseph R. Biden Jr. signed Executive Order 13990 on 'Protecting Public Health and the Environment and Restoring Science to Tackle the Climate Crisis.'  86 Fed. Reg. 7037 (Jan. 25, 2021).  That Executive Order and associated documents specifically directed EPA to review the 2020 Rule for compliance with the terms of the Executive Order.

3. Accordingly, on February 1, 2021, EPA moved for an order holding these consolidated cases in abeyance until 30 days after EPA completes its review of the 2020 rule and any resulting rulemaking.  ECF No. 1883156.  On February 12, 2021, the Court issued an Order that granted EPA's motion for abeyance, requiring that the agency file status reports at 120-day intervals and further requiring that the parties file a motion or motions to govern further proceedings within 30 days of the completion of agency proceedings.  ECF No. 1885114.

4. Since that date, EPA has continued to review the 2020 Rule as required under the Executive Order and in response to administrative reconsideration petitions it received in November 2020. 1 In addition, EPA has initiated public outreach to

6. In consideration of the above facts, EPA believes its ongoing review of the 2020 Rule justifies continued abeyance in the above-captioned case.
