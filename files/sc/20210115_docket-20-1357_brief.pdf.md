## INTRODUCTION

Section 7411 of the Clean Air Act authorizes EPA to set standards of performance governing air pollutant emissions from stationary sources, including sources in the oil and gas industry . Such standards are only authorized, however, where EPA has properly determined that emissions from a particular category of sources 'contribute significantly" to dangerous air pollution. In the Rule challenged here, EPA concluded that portions of its 2012 and 2016 performance standards for sources in the oil and gas industry failed this crucial test. EPA's determination was reasonable and its actions to amend or rescind the unlawful aspects of its rulemakings should be upheld. prior

The Rule under review made two course corrections.  First, EPA reasonably determined that transmission and storage facilities in the natural gas industry not be grouped in the same source category as production and processing facilities.   Transmission and storage facilities have different business purposes; use different processes; and together generate fewer emissions of a different mix than production and processing sources. As such, EPA reasonably concluded that its recent efforts to regulate all of these facilities in a single source category should be reversed. key

Second, EPA concluded that it had erred when it set methane standards for production and processing facilities. After a thorough reconsideration process,

-- Page Break --

EPA determined that those standards were the product of three distinct errors: (1) their promulgation was not an exercise of rational decision-making because are duplicative of standards already applicable to covered sources; (2) relied on à 'significant contribution? determination that considered emissions from the wrong set of sources; and (3) were promulgated without properly assessing when a source category' s contribution to dangerous pollution is "significant: they they they

Petitioners fail to demonstrate that EPA acted unreasonably or unlawfully when addressing these errors . their judgment for EPA's and to unduly accelerate the Agency's consideration of complex regulatory questions that it is working through in a measured, step-wise; and reasonable fashion.   Petitioners' arguments are unpersuasive. The Rule should be upheld.
